# Drifters

*The People of the Road*

Drifters have no single homeland.

Their communities are found wherever roads connect distant places: along trade routes, beside frontier settlements, in travelling caravans, and around temporary camps.

Some Drifters travel because they choose to.

Others travel because their families have done so for generations.

For many, remaining in one place for too long feels unnatural.

## Identity

Drifter identity is based on **movement, adaptation, and community** rather than appearance.

Two Drifters may look completely different and come from entirely different cultures.

What connects them is the belief that a person is shaped by the roads they travel and the people they meet.

Their cultures are therefore extremely diverse.

A Drifter caravan might preserve traditions hundreds of years old while another may develop an entirely new culture within a generation.

## The First Road

Many Drifter communities preserve a story known as **The First Road**.

The details vary between cultures, but the central idea remains the same:

> The first road was not built by stone. It was built by someone deciding to leave.

For Drifters, travel represents possibility.

The unknown is not necessarily something to fear.

It is something to discover.

## Stories at the Fire

Drifters place enormous value on storytelling.

When caravans stop for the night, stories are exchanged around fires, cooking stones, lanterns, or portable Resonance lamps.

Stories serve several purposes:

* Entertainment
* History
* Warnings
* Education
* Cultural preservation

A Drifter may know the history of a distant city without ever having visited it simply because its story has travelled through generations.

## The Empty Seat

Many Drifter caravans deliberately leave one place open around their evening gatherings.

This is called **The Empty Seat**.

It represents the stranger who has not arrived yet.

The tradition reflects a fundamental Drifter belief:

**Someone new will always enter your story.**

Refusing hospitality to a traveller is therefore considered a serious social offence in many communities.

## Relationship With Resonance

Drifters have no universal relationship with Resonance.

Their travelling lifestyle exposes them to many different Resonance environments.

A caravan travelling through a Fire-saturated region may adopt Fire-based crafts.

A coastal community may develop traditions surrounding Water.

A settlement near an old battlefield might develop practices involving Death Resonance.

Because of this, Drifter Resonance traditions tend to be practical rather than doctrinal.

They ask:

**"What does this place teach us?"**

rather than:

**"Which way is correct?"**

## Communities

Drifter society is decentralized.

There is no single Drifter government.

Instead, communities organize themselves around:

* Caravans
* Families
* Trade networks
* Guilds
* Travelling settlements
* Shared ancestry
* Shared routes

Some caravans are peaceful merchants.

Others are explorers.

Some specialize in dangerous salvage.

A few are little more than wandering criminal organizations.

## Relations With Other Lineages

Drifters commonly act as cultural intermediaries.

They are among the people most likely to have experience with multiple lineages and their customs.

This makes them useful negotiators and traders.

It can also make them outsiders everywhere.

A Drifter may be considered too foreign by settled communities and too settled by other travellers.

## Culture

Drifter culture commonly values:

* Adaptability
* Independence
* Hospitality
* Curiosity
* Exchange
* Practical knowledge
* Personal freedom
* Community

Their greatest cultural contradiction is that they value independence while depending heavily upon one another.

A Drifter may insist that they need nobody.

But when the caravan moves, everyone helps.

## Common Expressions

**"Keep the road open."**

A farewell meaning that the speaker hopes the traveller remains free.

**"The road remembers."**

A reminder that actions taken during travel eventually become stories.

**"Leave something useful behind."**

A cultural expectation that travellers should improve the places they pass through.

## Drifter Characters

A Drifter character can come from almost anywhere.

Their background may determine more about them than their lineage.

A Drifter could be:

* A merchant's child.
* A wandering scholar.
* A caravan guard.
* A travelling healer.
* A scavenger of ancient ruins.
* A courier.
* A mapmaker.
* A former criminal.
* A wandering Resonance practitioner.
* Someone who simply refuses to stay anywhere.

For a Drifter, the question is rarely:

**"Where are you from?"**

The more meaningful question is:

**"Where are you going?"**
