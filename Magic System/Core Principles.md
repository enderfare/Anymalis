# Glyphic Resonance — Core Principles

**Anymalis** is the system through which the world's magic is understood and structured.

Its magical phenomenon is commonly referred to as **Elemental Resonance**: invisible elemental currents that can be shaped through Glyphs, bodies, conductive materials, and specialized techniques.

A Glyph is a structured method of shaping Elemental Resonance. A Glyph is not inherently a spell; it becomes part of a spell when combined with the other required components of a construction.

---

## 1. The Basic Hierarchy

The fundamental relationship is:

```text
Element
└── Variant
```

An **Element** represents a fundamental principle.

A **Variant** is a specific expression of exactly one Element.

For example:

```text
Fire
├── Flame
├── Ember
├── Blaze
└── Spark
```

A Variant cannot belong to two Elements at once.

If a spell uses several Elements, each Element contributes its own Variants.

For example:

```text
Fire
└── Heat

Wind
└── Flow
```

is a multi-element construction, not a new hybrid Element.

---

## 2. Resonant Scars & Elemental Affinity

Every naturally magical individual is born with a natural glyph-like birthmark called a **Resonant Scar**.

The Scar normally encodes:

- one Element
- one specific Variant

This determines the character's natural Elemental Affinity.

### Affinity grants

- Lower Elemental Resonance consumption when using aligned magic.
- Easier spell stabilization.
- Greater precision.
- Instinctive understanding of the aligned Element.
- Greater resistance to backlash from aligned Elements.

A Resonant Scar may evolve through trauma, near-death experiences, ancient rituals, or Elemental Resonance overload.

---

# 3. Glyph Construction

A Glyph construction consists of several conceptual layers.

```text
Core Glyph
    ↓
Element
    ↓
Variant(s)
    ↓
Borders
    ↓
Foundation
    ↓
Conduction
    ↓
Surface
```

Not every character uses every physical component in the same way. Ink is the conventional conductor, while Animen and specialized techniques can provide alternative means of conduction.

---

## Core Glyph

The **Core Glyph** establishes which Element is being invoked.

It does not determine the entire effect of the spell.

A spell may contain more than one Element and therefore more than one Core Glyph.

Example:

```text
Fire → Heat
Water → Mist
```

Both Elements can participate in the same spell.

---

## Variants

A Variant determines which expression of an Element the Glyph manipulates.

Example:

```text
Fire → Heat
```

means the construction manipulates the Heat expression of Fire.

Variants are not generic actions.

For example, `Projectile` would not normally be a Fire Variant. A projectile is a form that a spell can take, while `Flame`, `Heat`, or `Ember` describe what Fire itself represents.

---

# 4. Borders

Borders determine how Elemental Resonance behaves within a construction.

Examples include:

| Border | Function |
|---|---|
| Foundation | Increases the conceptual importance of an existing Glyph |
| Flow | Controls directional movement |
| Amplification | Increases output intensity |
| Precision | Narrows or refines an effect |
| Duration | Maintains the effect over time |
| Trigger | Defines activation conditions |
| Containment | Prevents unwanted overflow |
| Transformation | Alters how the effect manifests |
| Connection | Links multiple Glyphs |
| Resonance | Synchronizes nearby Glyphs or effects |
| Echo | Repeats an effect after a delay |
| Anchor | Stabilizes the construction against external disruption |
| Veil | Conceals the Glyph or its manifestation |
| Pulse | Creates rhythmic activation |
| Fracture | Intentionally destabilizes Elemental Resonance |

### Foundation

Foundation does **not** determine an Element.

The Core Glyph already establishes the Element.

Instead:

> **Foundation increases the conceptual importance and presence of an existing Glyph within the construction.**

For example:

```text
Fire → Flame
+ Foundation
```

does not create Fire. It strengthens the role of the existing Fire Glyph in the construction.

---

# 5. Multi-Element Spells

A spell may contain more than one Element.

Each selected Element contributes one or more of its own Variants.

Example:

```text
Fire
└── Heat

Wind
└── Flow
```

The resulting spell may create a controlled stream of extremely hot air.

Another spell might use:

```text
Water
└── Flow

Time
└── Flow
```

The two `Flow` Variants are still distinct because each belongs to a different Element.

### Important rule

> A Variant always belongs to one Element. A Spell may combine multiple Elements.

---

# 6. Conductors

Elemental Resonance normally requires a medium through which a Glyph can be constructed or expressed.

The principal methods are:

### Conductive Ink

Traditional Glyph construction uses specialized conductive Ink.

### Animen

**Animen** is the personal magical resource available to users capable of directly manipulating Elemental Resonance.

Sufficient Animen allows certain users to construct or manifest spells without conductive Ink.

### Resonant Threads

Sericari can use their own Resonant Threads to construct or conduct Glyphs without conventional Ink.

These are alternative conduction methods, not separate Elements.

---

# 7. Conductive Ink

Normal ink cannot conduct Elemental Resonance.

Specialized conductive Inks include:

| Ink | Typical properties |
|---|---|
| Silverthread | Precision, stability, Time/Light compatibility |
| Ember Ash | Strong amplification, high backlash |
| Verdant Sap | Regeneration, long-duration organic effects |
| Hollow | Silence, concealment, resonance absorption |
| Glasswater | Reflection, duplication, repeating effects |

Ink is the conventional conductor for Glyphic construction, but it is not the only possible method.

---

# 8. Resonant Surfaces

A **Surface** is the physical medium upon which a Glyph is manifested or constructed.

The standard listed Surfaces are:

- Stone
- Skin
- Paper
- Cloth
- Metal
- Living Wood

These provide a medium for Glyph construction.

### Additional Surface Effects

Not every Surface grants an additional magical effect.

A Surface only provides a special mechanical or magical property when it is explicitly designated as a **Resonant Surface**.

Therefore:

```text
Stone
```

may simply provide a stable medium.

A specially prepared Resonant Surface might instead provide:

```text
Stone
→ Stability amplification
```

The object carrying the Surface does not change this rule. What matters is the actual Surface/material being used.

---

# 9. Spell Construction

A spell is a complete magical construction assembled from Elements, Variants, Borders, conduction methods, and, where applicable, a Surface.

### Example — Thermal Current

```text
Elements:
- Fire → Heat
- Wind → Flow

Borders:
- Flow
- Precision

Conductor:
- Ember Ash

Surface:
- Metal
```

**Effect:** A controlled stream of extremely hot air.

### Example — Temporal Tide

```text
Elements:
- Water → Flow
- Time → Flow

Borders:
- Flow
- Duration

Conductor:
- Silverthread

Surface:
- Cloth
```

**Effect:** A moving body of water whose temporal progression can be accelerated or slowed.

---

# 10. Animen Casting

A user with sufficient Animen may construct a spell without conductive Ink when their Path, abilities, or circumstances permit direct manipulation.

Example:

```text
Fire → Flame

Borders:
- Precision
- Transformation

Conductor:
- Animen
```

The spell is still a Fire construction. Animen simply replaces the conventional conductive medium.

---

# 11. Sericari Casting

A Sericari may use Resonant Threads as their conduction medium.

Example:

```text
Fire → Flame

Borders:
- Precision
- Duration

Conductor:
- Resonant Thread
```

No conventional Ink is required for the thread-based construction.

---

# 12. Glyph Fatigue

Glyph Fatigue represents the strain caused by maintaining and manipulating Elemental Resonance through a naturally magical body.

It is specifically associated with individuals born with a functional Resonant Scar.

Typical symptoms include:

- Shaking hands
- Blurred vision
- Glowing veins
- Temporary loss of Elemental control
- Physical or metaphysical instability

Extreme overuse may create **Hollows**.

Glyph Fatigue is separate from SAN.

---

# 13. SAN

**SAN** exists for all characters.

It represents mental and psychological stability.

SAN and Glyph Fatigue are separate systems and may change independently.

A traumatic event may reduce SAN without causing Glyph Fatigue.

Excessive Elemental Resonance use may increase Glyph Fatigue without reducing SAN.

A single event may affect both, but they should always be resolved separately.

---

# 14. Elements and Concentration

Elements with fewer Variants possess more concentrated Elemental Resonance.

This is primarily a conceptual property of the setting.

It does not mean that every spell from a low-Variant Element automatically deals more damage.

The number of Variants represents how broadly or narrowly an Element expresses itself.

---

# 15. Death and Nihil

**Death** governs the ending of things that exist.

**Nihil** represents the denial or cancellation of existence, definition, connection, or conceptual continuity.

Example:

```text
Death → End
```

can cause a flame to cease.

Nihil can instead attack the flame's existence or definition itself.

Death and Nihil should therefore remain conceptually distinct even when their results appear superficially similar.

---

# 16. Prima and Nihil

Prima and Nihil are opposing Meta-Glyphs.

Neither is inherently superior to the other.

When equivalent constructions of Prima and Nihil directly oppose one another, neither automatically overrides the other.

They enter a contest whose outcome depends on the actual constructions, circumstances, and resolution rules.

---

# 17. Design Rule for New Variants

A Variant should describe a fundamental expression of its Element.

Good:

```text
Fire → Flame
Fire → Heat
Water → Mist
Time → Cycle
```

Poor:

```text
Fire → Projectile
Fire → Attack
Fire → Shield
Fire → Explosion
```

The latter are normally spell forms or effects and should be produced through construction rather than added as Variants.

---

# 18. Examples of Invalid Construction

### Invalid Variant

```text
Fire → Mist
```

Invalid because Mist belongs to Water.

### Invalid hybrid Variant

```text
Fire/Water → Steam
```

Do not create a hybrid Element merely because two Elements interact.

Instead:

```text
Fire → Heat
Water → Mist
```

### Invalid Foundation usage

```text
Foundation → Fire
```

Foundation cannot create an Element.

It strengthens an existing Glyph.

---

## 19. Core Principle

The easiest way to understand Glyphic Resonance is:

```text
WHAT?
Element

WHICH EXPRESSION?
Variant

HOW DOES IT BEHAVE?
Borders

HOW IMPORTANT IS THE GLYPH?
Foundation

HOW IS IT CONDUCTED?
Ink / Animen / Resonant Threads

WHERE DOES IT MANIFEST?
Surface

WHAT DOES THE COMPLETE CONSTRUCTION DO?
Spell Effect
```
