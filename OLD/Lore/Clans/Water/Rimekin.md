# Rimekin

*The Frostbound Mischief-Makers*

The Rimekin are a people shaped by the coldest expressions of **Water Resonance**.

They inhabit places where winter is not a season but a way of life.

Frozen forests.

Glacier valleys.

Snow-covered mountains.

Icebound coasts.

Places where ordinary people might struggle to survive are home to the Rimekin.

Yet their culture is surprisingly lively.

Where others see a frozen wasteland, Rimekin see a playground.

## Water Made Winter

Rimekin philosophy begins with a simple idea:

**Frost is still water.**

It has simply become something else.

This concept influences how they understand both Resonance and life.

Water flows.

Water changes.

Water freezes.

None of these states are considered fundamentally different.

They are simply different expressions of the same thing.

Because of this, Rimekin scholars often describe Frost as a lesson in adaptation.

## The Winter People

Rimekin settlements are rarely large.

Most are small communities scattered across frozen regions.

Homes are commonly constructed from:

* Packed snow.
* Ice.
* Stone.
* Bone.
* Frost-resistant timber.
* Resonance-infused materials.

Some settlements are built beneath glaciers.

Others are carved into mountains.

Some appear to be nothing more than collections of snow-covered caves until approached.

## The First Frost

Rimekin children traditionally undergo a ceremony called **The First Frost**.

During the ceremony, the child is brought outside during the first significant frost of the season.

They are given a small bowl of water.

The child is then encouraged to freeze it using their natural Resonance.

The shape of the resulting ice is carefully observed.

Not because it predicts their future.

Rather, it represents their first deliberate act of shaping the world.

The resulting piece of ice is often kept by the family.

## Frost Games

Rimekin children and adults participate in elaborate games involving snow and ice.

These games are more than entertainment.

They teach:

* Balance.
* Movement.
* Awareness.
* Creativity.
* Resonance control.
* Cooperation.

Some communities hold enormous annual Frost Games where entire settlements compete.

The games may include:

* Ice races.
* Snow mazes.
* Frost sculpture contests.
* Sliding competitions.
* Snowball duels.
* Hidden-object hunts.

Winning is appreciated.

Creativity is respected more.

## Mischief

Rimekin are famous for their pranks.

Their culture considers harmless mischief a form of social expression.

A clever prank demonstrates that someone is observant enough to find an opportunity and creative enough to exploit it.

But there is an important boundary.

**Mischief should leave laughter behind.**

If someone is genuinely hurt, frightened, or humiliated, the prank has crossed the line.

## The Silent Snow

One of the oldest Rimekin traditions is known as **The Silent Snow**.

Once each year, a community spends an entire night without speaking.

Everyone gathers outside beneath the snow.

No music.

No conversation.

No machinery.

Only the sound of the environment.

The tradition teaches the other side of winter.

Stillness.

Patience.

Listening.

For a people known for laughter and mischief, silence is considered an important discipline.

## Relationship With Frost Resonance

Rimekin have a natural affinity for **Water → Frost**.

However, they do not claim ownership over the Frost Variant.

Other peoples can learn Frost Resonance.

Rimekin simply possess a particularly strong cultural and biological relationship with it.

This distinction is important to their scholars.

They often say:

> **"The snow belongs to no one."**

## Relationship With Warmth

Rimekin are not inherently hostile to heat.

In fact, warmth is culturally important.

Communal fires, heated stones, warm food, and shared shelters are often associated with friendship and safety.

A warm room during a blizzard is considered one of the greatest luxuries in Rimekin culture.

Their relationship with Fire is therefore not necessarily antagonistic.

Fire and Frost represent opposing conditions, but both provide valuable lessons.

## Culture

Rimekin culture commonly values:

* Playfulness
* Curiosity
* Cleverness
* Patience
* Adaptability
* Community
* Endurance
* Freedom

They tend to dislike excessive formality.

A Rimekin who takes themselves too seriously may quickly become the target of a prank.

## Names

Rimekin names often have short, playful sounds.

Common naming themes involve:

* Snow.
* Frost.
* Wind.
* Ice.
* Animals.
* Winter phenomena.

Some communities give children temporary names based on their personality.

A child who constantly disappears might be called **Little Shadow**.

A child who never stops moving might become **Snowrunner**.

These names sometimes remain as adult nicknames.

## Relations With Other Peoples

Rimekin are generally curious about outsiders.

Travellers are often welcomed into their settlements, particularly during winter.

They have strong traditions of guiding travellers through dangerous frozen terrain.

Their playful reputation can sometimes make outsiders underestimate them.

Rimekin are accustomed to surviving environments that would kill an unprepared traveller.

## Character Hooks

A Rimekin character might be:

* A wandering Frost Resonance practitioner.
* A scout from a remote glacier settlement.
* A famous prankster trying to mature.
* A hunter searching for a legendary winter creature.
* A guide who knows forgotten paths beneath the ice.
* An explorer fascinated by warmer parts of the world.
* A young Rimekin who has discovered an unusual Frost Variant expression.

## The Rimekin View of Winter

To the Rimekin, winter is not simply an obstacle.

It is a teacher.

It teaches patience.

It teaches preparation.

It teaches movement.

It teaches stillness.

And sometimes, it teaches you to laugh when you fall face-first into the snow.

> **"They call it surviving winter. We call it living."**
