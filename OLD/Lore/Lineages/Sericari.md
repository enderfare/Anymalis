# Sericari

*The Silkbound Artisans*

The Sericari are an ancient people whose civilization grew around a simple discovery:

**Silk could carry Resonance.**

What began as a biological trait became a craft.

The craft became a tradition.

The tradition became the foundation of their civilization.

## The Silkbound

To the Sericari, silk is more than a material.

It is a connection between the body and the world.

A strand can carry heat.

A woven pattern can preserve a Glyph.

A garment can contain generations of craftsmanship.

A bridge can become a Resonance structure.

Their oldest traditions describe silk as the **thread between self and world**.

## The First Thread

Sericari children are traditionally taught to produce and manipulate silk at a young age.

The first successful strand is called the **First Thread**.

Parents often preserve it as part of the child's personal history.

Some families weave the thread into clothing.

Others incorporate it into a family tapestry.

Among some communities, the First Thread is eventually used in the individual's most important personal possession.

## Silk-Lineages

Sericari society is divided into **Silk-Lineages**.

These are not simply families.

A Silk-Lineage represents generations of adaptation to a particular Resonance environment.

Over time, prolonged exposure to an Element can influence:

* Biology
* Silk properties
* Architecture
* Clothing
* Religion
* Craft
* Food
* Social customs
* Philosophy

As a result, different Silk-Lineages can feel almost like separate cultures while still sharing a common Sericari identity.

## Cultivating Resonance

The Sericari philosophy toward Resonance is often summarized as:

**"Do not command the thread. Learn how it wants to be woven."**

Where other cultures may attempt to impose their will upon Resonance, Sericari traditions emphasize cultivation.

They observe.

They experiment.

They adapt.

They allow the environment to influence the craft.

This does not mean Sericari Resonance techniques are passive.

A master weaver may produce extraordinary effects.

But the process is treated as a relationship rather than domination.

## Silk and the Elements

Every major Silk-Lineage is associated with an Element.

Fire-affiliated Sericari may develop heat-resistant silk.

Water-affiliated Sericari may produce silk capable of remaining flexible underwater.

Earth-affiliated Sericari may create exceptionally dense structural fibers.

Wind-affiliated Sericari may produce exceptionally light silk.

The possibilities are shaped by the Element and its Resonance.

However, the Element is not the same thing as the individual's Variant.

Two Sericari belonging to the same Silk-Lineage may express very different Resonance techniques.

## The Living Loom

The most important structure in many Sericari settlements is the **Living Loom**.

A Living Loom is more than a weaving device.

It is a place where:

* Silk is produced.
* Resonance is cultivated.
* Glyphs are woven.
* History is recorded.
* Apprentices are trained.
* Important communal objects are created.

Some ancient Living Looms are believed to have been operating for centuries.

## Architecture

Sericari architecture tends toward vertical and interconnected structures.

Buildings may be connected by:

* Silk bridges
* Suspended walkways
* Woven platforms
* Resonance threads
* Large communal webs

Caverns and forests are particularly common locations for Sericari settlements because both environments provide abundant places to anchor large structures.

## Clothing

Sericari clothing is often highly individualized.

A person's clothing can communicate:

* Silk-Lineage
* Family
* Profession
* Skill
* Social status
* Personal achievements

Master artisans may wear garments containing thousands of individually woven strands.

Some ceremonial clothing is itself a Resonance artifact.

## Society

Sericari communities commonly organize around craft.

Important figures may include:

* Master Weavers
* Resonance Cultivators
* Silk-Lineage Elders
* Glyphwrights
* Artificers
* Loom Keepers

However, no single political structure governs every Sericari.

Different Silk-Lineages maintain their own traditions and forms of leadership.

## The Philosophy of Cultivation

Sericari philosophy centers on three ideas:

**Observe.**

Understand the Resonance around you.

**Cultivate.**

Work with it rather than against it.

**Weave.**

Turn understanding into something that can endure.

These principles influence nearly every aspect of Sericari culture.

## Relations With Other Lineages

Sericari frequently trade with other peoples.

Their silk is valuable throughout the world because of its strength and Resonance-conductive properties.

They are particularly respected by Artificers and Scribes.

Some Sericari communities are cautious around cultures that attempt to force Resonance into unnatural forms.

The memory of the **Veil War** has reinforced this caution.

## The Veil War

The Sericari remember the Veil War differently depending on their Silk-Lineage.

Some believe the war demonstrated the danger of trying to force Resonance beyond its natural limits.

Others believe the lessons of the war should encourage greater understanding rather than fear.

Nearly all agree on one principle:

**Resonance cannot be treated as a simple tool.**

It changes what works with it.

## Sericari Culture

Sericari commonly value:

* Craftsmanship
* Patience
* Knowledge
* Adaptation
* Community
* Creation
* Resonance harmony

A beautiful object is respected.

A useful object is respected.

But an object that is both beautiful **and** useful is considered exceptional.

## Common Expressions

**"Every thread remembers."**

A reminder that craftsmanship carries history.

**"Weave before you cut."**

A warning against destroying something before understanding its purpose.

**"The thread follows the hand."**

A saying about mastery: skill determines what potential becomes.

**"Find your loom."**

A phrase meaning to find one's place, craft, or purpose.

## Sericari Characters

A Sericari character should have three layers of identity:

**Sericari**

Their shared ancestry, silk biology, and cultural foundations.

**Silk-Lineage**

The Element and culture that shaped them.

**Individual**

Their Resonant Scar, Variant, Path, skills, beliefs, and personal history.

This allows two Sericari characters to feel related without feeling identical.

> **"The world is not woven from one thread."**
