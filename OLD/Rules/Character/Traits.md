# Traits

Traits are persistent characteristics that meaningfully distinguish a character beyond their Attributes, Skills, Path, Background, and Techniques.

A Trait may represent a biological feature, learned disposition, supernatural condition, cultural characteristic, unusual sensory capability, or other persistent aspect of the character.

Traits describe **what is inherently or persistently true about a character**.

They do not replace Skills or Techniques.

---

## Trait Categories

Traits may belong to one or more of the following categories.

### Biological

A physical or biological characteristic of the character.

Examples:

* Unusual anatomy
* Natural adaptations
* Enhanced senses
* Innate physiological properties

### Cultural

A characteristic developed through upbringing, community, tradition, or shared identity.

Examples:

* Cultural customs
* Social instincts
* Traditional knowledge
* Community-specific behavior

### Resonant

A characteristic produced by the character's relationship with Resonance.

Examples:

* Unusual Resonance perception
* Persistent elemental manifestations
* Resonance-related adaptations

A Resonant Trait does not automatically grant an Elemental Affinity.

Elemental Affinity is determined by the character's **Resonant Scar** when applicable.

### Supernatural

A persistent characteristic resulting from unusual magical, metaphysical, or other supernatural circumstances.

Examples:

* Magical alterations
* Persistent blessings
* Curses
* Unusual metaphysical states

### Social

A persistent characteristic primarily affecting how the character interacts with or is perceived by others.

Examples:

* Recognizable cultural markers
* Unusual presence
* Social expectations associated with an identity

---

# Trait Sources

A character may acquire Traits from multiple sources.

Common sources include:

* Lineage
* Lineage Specialization
* Background
* Path
* Conditions
* Advancement
* Story events
* Supernatural transformation

A Trait should identify its source when the source matters mechanically or narratively.

---

# Trait Structure

A Trait should normally contain the following information:

```text
Name
Category
Source
Description
Mechanical Effect
Limitations
```

Not every Trait requires a mechanical effect.

Some Traits exist primarily to establish persistent narrative facts about a character.

---

## Description

The Description explains what the Trait represents.

It should establish the character's persistent characteristic without unnecessarily prescribing behavior or personality.

A Trait should describe a **capability, property, condition, or identity**, rather than a complete character concept.

---

## Mechanical Effect

When a Trait has a mechanical effect, that effect should be specific.

A Trait may:

* Modify how a Skill is used
* Grant a narrow capability
* Allow access to a specific interaction
* Alter how a character responds to a circumstance
* Provide a limited passive benefit
* Create a meaningful narrative permission
* Establish a special interaction with Resonance or another system

Traits should not duplicate the function of Techniques.

If an ability requires deliberate activation, a meaningful action, or a defined sequence of choices, it should generally be represented as a **Technique** instead.

---

## Limitations

Traits should have clear boundaries when their effects could otherwise become ambiguous.

A limitation may define:

* Required circumstances
* Range
* Frequency
* Applicable Skills
* Applicable targets
* Environmental requirements
* Situations in which the Trait does not apply

Limitations are part of the Trait rather than an optional excuse for restricting it during play.

---

# Trait and Skill Relationship

Traits and Skills represent different layers of character capability.

**Skill** describes what the character has learned to do.

**Trait** describes a persistent characteristic that affects what the character is or how their capabilities function.

A Trait may interact with a Skill without replacing it.

For example:

> A character may possess a Trait that allows them to perceive vibrations through a particular biological structure.

The Trait establishes the sensory capability.

A Skill is still used when the character attempts to interpret those vibrations for a specific task.

---

# Trait and Technique Relationship

Traits are generally persistent.

Techniques are generally deliberate.

A Trait may establish that a character possesses a particular capability, while a Technique defines how the character deliberately applies that capability.

For example:

**Trait: Resonant Hearing**

The character can perceive certain Resonance disturbances through sound.

**Technique: Resonance Echo**

The character deliberately focuses this perception to locate the source of a Resonance disturbance.

The Trait establishes the underlying capability.

The Technique establishes the specialized application.

---

# Lineage Traits

A Lineage may provide a Lineage Trait as part of its standard character package.

A Lineage Trait should express something meaningfully associated with that lineage.

It should not attempt to represent everything that makes the lineage unique.

Lineage Traits should generally describe:

* Biology
* Sensory characteristics
* Cultural characteristics
* Environmental adaptations
* Persistent identity-linked properties

Lineage Traits should not normally determine:

* Elemental Affinity
* Element
* Variant
* Path
* Complete combat role
* Entire skill set

These belong to other character systems.

---

# Trait Stacking

Traits may interact with one another.

When multiple Traits appear to grant the same capability, they do not automatically stack.

Use the following order:

1. Determine whether the Traits provide different capabilities.
2. If they provide different capabilities, both apply.
3. If they provide the same capability, use the more specific rule.
4. If neither Trait is more specific, apply the normal rule only once unless a rule explicitly permits stacking.

A Trait should never create an unintended numerical escalation simply because another Trait uses similar wording.

---

# Trait Changes

Traits may change during a character's life.

A Trait can:

* Develop
* Be suppressed
* Be altered
* Be lost
* Be replaced
* Gain additional effects

Such changes should have a meaningful cause when they are part of the fiction.

Possible causes include:

* Training
* Injury
* Magical exposure
* Resonance overload
* Transformation
* Ritual
* Advancement
* Major life events

A changed Trait should remain consistent with the cause that produced the change.

---

# Conditions and Traits

Not every persistent condition is a Trait.

Conditions such as Afflictions should remain separate when they represent a state imposed upon or experienced by the character rather than an inherent character feature.

For example:

* A biological sensory adaptation may be a Trait.
* A magical affliction that alters perception may be a Condition.
* A permanent transformation resulting from that affliction may eventually become a Trait.

The distinction should be based on what the rule represents, not simply whether the effect is permanent.

---

# Trait Design Principles

A good Trait should answer at least one of these questions:

* What is unusual about this character?
* What persistent capability does this character possess?
* What biological characteristic affects their life?
* What cultural characteristic follows them?
* What persistent supernatural property defines part of their identity?
* What meaningful interaction does this character have with the world?

A Trait should **not** answer every character-design question at once.

Traits are building blocks.

They work alongside:

```text
Attributes
Skills
Traits
Techniques
Specializations
Backgrounds
Lineage
Path
Resonant Scar
Conditions
```

No single category should be responsible for defining the entire character.

---

# Trait Design Test

Before adding a Trait, ask:

### 1. Is it persistent?

If the ability only exists when deliberately activated, it may belong as a Technique instead.

### 2. Is it distinct?

If another existing system already represents the same thing, determine whether the Trait is actually necessary.

### 3. Is it specific?

Avoid Traits whose wording could reasonably apply to almost any situation.

### 4. Does it have a clear source?

Know whether the Trait comes from Lineage, Background, Path, Condition, advancement, or another source.

### 5. Does it preserve character agency?

A Trait should establish capabilities or circumstances without dictating every decision the character makes.

### 6. Does it create unintended system overlap?

Check Skills, Techniques, Resonant Scars, Conditions, and Path abilities before creating a new mechanical effect.

---

# Standard Trait Format

Use the following structure when creating a Trait:

```text
# [Trait Name]

## Category
[Biological / Cultural / Resonant / Supernatural / Social]

## Source
[Lineage / Background / Path / Condition / Advancement / Other]

## Description
[What the Trait represents.]

## Mechanical Effect
[What the Trait does mechanically, if applicable.]

## Limitations
[Relevant boundaries, requirements, or exceptions.]
```

Traits should remain concise enough that their function can be understood without consulting unrelated character rules.

The complete character context comes from the interaction of multiple systems rather than from increasingly large individual Traits.
