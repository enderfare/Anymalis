# Reputation

Reputation is a social progression system independent of Skills, Attributes, Paths, and Resonance.

```text
Skills     = what you can do
Renown     = how many people know you
Reputation = what they think about you
Tags       = what they associate with you
Rumors     = what they believe about you
```

Reputation changes through events, achievements, failures, public actions, rumors, and relationships.

## Reputation Scale

| Score | Rank | Meaning |
|---:|---|---|
| -5 | Infamous | Strong hostility or fear |
| -4 | Hated | Deeply negative |
| -3 | Disgraced | Known for serious wrongdoing/failure |
| -2 | Suspect | Strongly wary |
| -1 | Unfavorable | Mildly negative |
| 0 | Neutral | No meaningful opinion |
| +1 | Familiar | Mildly positive |
| +2 | Respected | Generally trusted |
| +3 | Renowned | Strong positive standing |
| +4 | Celebrated | Exceptional standing |
| +5 | Legendary | Extremely influential |

Reputation is contextual.

A character may have:

```text
Public: +3
Royal Court: -2
Artificer Guild: +4
Underworld: +1
```

## Reputation Tracks

Common tracks:

- Public.
- Professional.
- Faction.
- Personal.

Only important relationships need Personal Reputation tracking.

## Reputation Checks

When a reputation is known and relevant:

```text
d20
+ Attribute Modifier
+ Skill Mastery
+ Specialization
+ Reputation Modifier
+ Situational Modifiers
```

A Reputation Tag normally grants +2 when directly relevant.

## Social Permission

High reputation can grant access without giving a numerical bonus:

- Audience with important people.
- Restricted events.
- Professional opportunities.
- Introductions.
- Favors.
- Sensitive information.
- Assistance.

## Negative Reputation

Negative Reputation can be useful when people expect the character to be dangerous.

It may help with intimidation, criminal contacts, or hostile negotiations while harming relations with authorities and civilians.

Therefore:

```text
Reputation = Social Expectation
```

not simply good/bad modifiers.

## Events

Reputation changes through:

```text
ACTION
↓
CONSEQUENCE
↓
WITNESSES
↓
STORY
↓
RUMOR
↓
REPUTATION
```

The same event may improve one group's opinion and damage another's.

## Loss and Recovery

Reputation may fall through meaningful public failures, betrayal, crimes, exposed lies, broken promises, or public defeat.

It may recover through meaningful corrective action such as restitution, rescue, apology, exposing false accusations, or rebuilding trust.

Waiting alone normally does not restore lost reputation.
