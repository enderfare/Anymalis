# Drifters

*The People of the Road*

Drifters are defined less by blood than by movement.

They travel between settlements, regions, cultures, and Resonance zones, carrying stories, goods, techniques, and traditions with them.

A Drifter may belong to a caravan, a travelling family, a merchant road, a wandering community, or simply a loose network of people who refuse to remain in one place.

Drifter identity is therefore shaped as much by culture and experience as by ancestry.

---

## Identity

Drifters do not possess a single homeland or universal way of life.

Their communities may form around:

* Travelling caravans
* Merchant routes
* Frontier settlements
* Wandering families
* Trade networks
* Pilgrimages
* Exploration
* Seasonal migration

Different Drifter communities can develop substantially different customs, traditions, and practical skills.

A Drifter may travel continuously, travel seasonally, or eventually settle while retaining their cultural identity.

Being a Drifter does not require a particular profession, personality, morality, or Path.

---

## Skill Training

### Roadwise

Drifters commonly develop practical knowledge of travel, unfamiliar environments, and the communities connected by the road.

The standard Drifter Lineage Skill Training is:

**Survival**

This represents familiarity with:

* Routes
* Weather
* Campsites
* Travel hazards
* Terrain
* Resource management
* Signs of habitation
* Practical navigation

Individual Drifter communities may replace this Skill Training with another appropriate Skill when their traditions strongly emphasize a different capability.

---

## Lineage Trait

### Adaptable

Drifters are accustomed to entering unfamiliar environments and adjusting to changing circumstances.

Their communities commonly exchange practical knowledge between regions, allowing Drifters to learn from the people and environments they encounter.

**Mechanical Effect:** A Drifter gains familiarity with unfamiliar customs, practical techniques, and environmental practices more readily than someone without comparable experience.

When the character enters a new community or region, the GM may allow this Trait to reduce the difficulty or time required to understand ordinary local practices.

The Trait does not grant automatic fluency, expertise, or knowledge of specialized subjects.

It does not replace an appropriate Skill check.

---

## Lineage Technique

### Improvisation

Drifters rarely have exactly what they need.

Instead, they learn to make useful things from whatever happens to be available.

**Mechanical Effect:** Once per rest, a Drifter may improvise a simple mundane tool, object, or temporary practical solution from available materials, subject to GM approval.

The improvised result must be reasonable given the available materials and circumstances.

It cannot:

* Create complex machinery from unsuitable materials
* Reproduce specialized equipment the character does not understand
* Create magical items
* Create weapons or tools whose complexity exceeds the character's relevant knowledge
* Permanently replace specialized equipment

The Technique represents practical improvisation rather than unrestricted fabrication.

---

## Roadwise Technique

The old Drifter rules included **Roadwise** as a separate mechanical ability.

Under the new modular model, its core concept is retained without making it a second Lineage Trait.

### Roadwise

A Drifter has developed an instinct for routes, settlements, weather, danger, and the subtle signs that distinguish a safe road from a dangerous one.

**Mechanical Effect:** When using an appropriate Skill to:

* Navigate unfamiliar territory
* Identify common travel hazards
* Determine a practical route
* Interpret ordinary signs of travel

the Drifter may apply the benefits provided by this Technique when the circumstances fall within their travel experience.

The Technique does not provide supernatural navigation and does not reveal unknown locations automatically.

Its effectiveness depends on available environmental information and the character's relevant Skill.

## Lineage Specializations

Drifter communities may develop specialized traditions based on their routes, environments, and relationships.

Possible Specializations include:

* Caravan Travel
* Wilderness Travel
* Urban Travel
* River Travel
* Coastal Travel
* Mountain Travel
* Trade Routes
* Storykeeping
* Hospitality
* Scouting
* Mapping
* Salvage

These are examples rather than a universal list.

A Drifter Specialization should represent a meaningful tradition or field of experience within a particular Drifter community.

---

## Resonance

Unlike many established Lineages, Drifters possess no universal Elemental Affinity.

Their strength comes from exposure and adaptation.

A Drifter raised beside Fire Resonance may learn Fire techniques.

Another raised among Water Resonance may develop traditions surrounding Water.

Others may never develop a natural Elemental Affinity at all.

This makes Drifters particularly varied in their relationship with Glyphic Resonance.

### Resonant Scar

A naturally magical Drifter possesses a Resonant Scar.

The Resonant Scar determines the individual's Element and Variant according to the Resonance rules.

Drifter culture does not determine the character's Element or Variant.

A Drifter's community may influence:

* Which Resonance techniques they are taught
* Which Elements are culturally familiar
* Which Glyphic traditions they encounter
* Which Resonant materials are commonly available

These cultural influences do not replace the individual's Resonant Scar.

---

## Resonance Practices

Drifter communities frequently encounter other cultures and magical traditions.

As a result, a Drifter may learn Resonance practices from multiple regions during their lifetime.

This can produce characters who possess unusually broad cultural knowledge of magical traditions.

Learning a technique associated with an Element does not automatically grant Elemental Affinity with that Element.

The character must satisfy the requirements of the relevant Technique or Magic System rule.

---

## Paths

Drifters can follow any of the Four Paths:

* **Scribe**
* **Channeler**
* **Artificer**
* **Null**

The Lineage places no Path restriction on a Drifter.

Common examples include:

### Scribes

Travelling glyphwrights, mapmakers, historians, ritualists, and keepers of written traditions.

### Channelers

Scouts, hunters, caravan guards, messengers, and travelling protectors.

### Artificers

Mechanics, salvagers, engineers, repairers, and travelling craftsmen.

### Nulls

Rare wanderers who specialize in surviving or travelling through dangerous Resonance environments using Null techniques or characteristics.

These are cultural examples, not mechanical restrictions.

---

## Biological Notes

Drifters do not possess a single universal physical appearance.

Their populations may vary significantly due to ancestry, migration, inter-community relationships, and environmental adaptation.

Commonly identified characteristics include:

* Highly varied skin colours
* Highly varied hair colours
* Highly varied eye colours
* Travel-worn equipment
* Community-specific markings

There is no universal physical feature that identifies every Drifter.

### ATS

The existing Drifter rules specify:

```text
ATS Base: +2 ATS
```

ATS is a biological statistic and is therefore not treated as a standard Lineage Trait.

Its application remains governed by the biological rules.

---

## Attribute Bonuses

The previous Drifter rules contained:

```text
+1 Insight
+1 Agility
```

Insight remains a current Attribute.

**Agility is not one of the current six Attributes.**

The current Attribute system is:

```text
Might
Finesse
Endurance
Intellect
Insight
Presence
```

Therefore the Agility bonus cannot be migrated automatically.

Until the Attribute system establishes what Agility represents or how the old bonus is translated, the legacy Agility bonus should remain unresolved rather than being silently converted into Finesse or another Attribute.

The existing +1 Insight bonus should likewise remain subject to the broader decision about whether Lineages still provide direct Attribute bonuses.

No new Attribute bonus is created by this file.

---

## Glyphic Ink

The previous Drifter rules specify:

```text
Glyphic Ink Bonus: +0
```

This does not provide a positive Lineage bonus.

It therefore does not need to create a new Lineage mechanic.

Drifters may use Glyphic Ink normally according to the general Ink rules.

Their cultural access to materials may vary by community and location.

## Common Roles

Drifters may commonly become:

* Merchant
* Guide
* Scout
* Courier
* Explorer
* Salvager
* Caravan Guard
* Storyteller
* Mapmaker
* Smuggler
* Wanderer

These are cultural and occupational examples, not mechanical restrictions.

A Drifter can pursue any appropriate profession.

---

## Community-Dependent Sub-Lineages

Drifter communities may develop distinct identities without necessarily becoming separate Lineages.

These differences should normally be represented through:

* Specializations
* Backgrounds
* Skills
* Traits
* Techniques
* Community Lore

A community should become a distinct Lineage only when its biological or cultural identity is sufficiently distinct to justify a separate Lineage entry.

---

## Character Hooks

A Drifter character might:

* Be searching for a permanent home.
* Carry an important message between distant settlements.
* Have learned Resonance techniques from several cultures.
* Be following an old road that no longer appears on modern maps.
* Have inherited a strange object from a travelling ancestor.
* Know stories about places that most people believe never existed.
* Have left a caravan under unusual circumstances.
* Be responsible for repaying an old debt between travelling communities.
* Be searching for a person they met only once on the road.
* Have knowledge of a route that has disappeared from modern maps.

---

## Character Considerations

Drifter identity should establish cultural context without dictating:

* Personality
* Morality
* Profession
* Path
* Elemental Affinity
* Political beliefs
* Personal goals

Two Drifters may have almost nothing else in common beyond their shared cultural identity.

Their individual histories should determine how that identity manifests.
